# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Two groups share one product.

Brand marketing leads at small Indian D2C brands (5 to 50 people, a team of 1 to 3 running creator campaigns). They work in short bursts between launches, often on a laptop with WhatsApp and a spreadsheet open next to it. They run 7 to 30 creators a month, mostly nano and micro, paid in cash with some barter. Their job is to pick creators, get posts live on time, and know afterwards whether the money worked.

Nano and micro creators (roughly 2K to 100K followers), mostly on their phones. They want to find brand work, agree a fair fee, know exactly what to deliver, and get paid on time.

## Product Purpose
nano.club is a creator marketplace and campaign tracker. Brands find creators, run a campaign, and see per post what each creator delivered against the views the brand paid for, plus sales through creator codes. Creators see open campaigns, apply or accept invitations, and submit their posts.

Success: a brand lead can answer "did I get the views I paid for, creator by creator, and can I trust these numbers?" in ten seconds, and chooses next month's creators from that history.

The prototype shows sample data only. Reach, saves and shares are private to creators, so the product always shows where each number came from.

## Brand Personality
Approachable, playful, honest. Small creators, real numbers.

The voice is plain and friendly, never hyped. It talks like a sharp friend who runs creator campaigns, not like a growth agency. Playful in colour and shape, exact in the numbers.

## Anti-references
- Purple SaaS dashboards: gradient purple, identical rounded metric tiles.
- Influencer-agency hype: neon, glitter, emoji, "go viral" energy.
- Enterprise grey admin tools in the style of the large influencer suites.
- Cream and brass boutique: warm paper backgrounds with gold accents.

Reference for feel: CRED (confident, high contrast, Indian) and Spotify or Instagram (creator culture, bold colour blocks, big avatars). Positioned against Modash, Qoruz, Kofluence and similar suites, which lean blue or purple and enterprise.

## Design Principles
1. Show where a number came from. Every figure carries its source, and private data is shown as private, never guessed.
2. Paid versus delivered is the headline. Lead with what the brand paid for and what arrived.
3. Friendly shell, exact data. Marketplace and creator screens can be warm and playful. The performance grid stays calm, dense and readable.
4. Small creators are the stars. Avatars, names and track records get room.
5. Say plainly what is a mock. The prototype never implies live data or real connections.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG 2.1 AA: text contrast of 4.5:1 or better, visible focus states, keyboard access for all actions, and reduced motion respected. Colour is never the only signal for delivery status. Many users are on mid-range Android phones and mobile data, so layouts work at 375px wide and pages stay light. Interface language is English with Indian number formats.
